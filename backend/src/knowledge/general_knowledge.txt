=== ODISHA HISTORY AND GEOGRAPHY ===
Odisha (formerly Orissa) is located on the eastern coast of India. The Bay of Bengal borders it to the east. The capital city is Bhubaneswar (the Temple City). Cuttack is the judicial capital and former capital. Major rivers: Mahanadi, Brahmani, Baitarani, Subarnarekha. The Chilika Lake is Asia's largest brackish water lagoon and a Ramsar site. Odisha is divided into 30 districts. The state language is Odia, one of the classical languages of India recognised by the Government of India in 2014. Odisha has the largest tribal population in India. Major tribes: Kondhs, Santals, Gonds, Saoras. Key festivals: Rath Yatra (Jagannath Temple, Puri), Raja, Nuakhai, Durga Puja, Diwali. The Konark Sun Temple (13th century, built by Narasimhadeva I) and the Lingaraja Temple in Bhubaneswar are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

=== INDIAN HISTORY — KEY EVENTS ===
The Indus Valley Civilisation (3000-1500 BCE) had well-planned cities like Mohenjo-daro and Harappa. The Vedic period followed (1500-600 BCE). Maurya Empire: founded by Chandragupta Maurya (321 BCE). Emperor Ashoka after the Kalinga War (261 BCE, fought in modern Odisha) adopted Buddhism and preached non-violence. Gupta Empire (320-550 CE) is called the Golden Age of India — advances in science, mathematics, art. Medieval India: Delhi Sultanate (1206-1526), Mughal Empire (1526-1857). The British East India Company arrived in 1600. First War of Independence (1857). Indian National Congress founded (1885). Key freedom fighters: Mahatma Gandhi (non-cooperation, civil disobedience, Quit India), Subhas Chandra Bose (INA), Bhagat Singh, Sardar Patel. India got independence on 15 August 1947. Republic Day is 26 January 1950 — when the Constitution of India came into force.

=== INDIAN CONSTITUTION AND GOVERNMENT ===
India is a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic republic (words from Preamble). Three branches: Legislature (Parliament: Lok Sabha + Rajya Sabha), Executive (President, Vice-President, Prime Minister, Council of Ministers), Judiciary (Supreme Court, High Courts, District Courts). Fundamental Rights: Right to Equality, Right to Freedom, Right against Exploitation, Right to Freedom of Religion, Cultural and Educational Rights, Right to Constitutional Remedies. Fundamental Duties are listed in Article 51A. The President is the constitutional head; the Prime Minister is the head of the government. Members of Lok Sabha are directly elected (545 seats total). Rajya Sabha has 250 members (elected by state legislatures + 12 nominated by President).

=== ECONOMICS BASICS ===
GDP (Gross Domestic Product) is the total value of goods and services produced in a country in a year. Primary sector: agriculture, fishing, mining. Secondary sector: manufacturing, industry. Tertiary sector: services (education, banking, IT). India is a mixed economy (private + public sector). Green Revolution (1960s): introduced high-yielding variety seeds, increased food production (Punjab, Haryana). White Revolution (Operation Flood): increased milk production. National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (NREGA) provides 100 days of guaranteed employment. Consumer rights: right to safety, right to information, right to choose, right to be heard, right to redress, right to consumer education. Consumer courts: District Forum (up to Rs 20 lakh), State Commission (up to Rs 1 crore), National Commission (above Rs 1 crore).

=== GEOGRAPHY — PHYSICAL FEATURES OF INDIA ===
India has six major physiographic regions: Himalayan Mountains, Northern Plains, Peninsular Plateau, Indian Desert (Thar), Coastal Plains, Island Groups. The Himalayas have three ranges: Greater Himalayas (Himadri), Middle Himalayas (Himachal), Outer Himalayas (Shiwaliks). Highest peak in India: Kangchenjunga (8586 m). The Indo-Gangetic Plain (Northern Plains) is the most fertile region, formed by alluvial deposits of Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra. The Deccan Plateau is a triangular shaped plateau. The Western Ghats are continuous; Eastern Ghats are discontinuous. Rivers from Himalayan systems are perennial (fed by glaciers + rain). Peninsula rivers are seasonal (fed mainly by rain). Monsoon: South-West monsoon (June-September) brings most of India's rainfall.

=== ENVIRONMENTAL STUDIES ===
Biodiversity is the variety of life on Earth — genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity. India is a mega-biodiversity country with 7-8% of world's species. Biosphere reserves protect biodiversity (e.g., Simlipal in Odisha). Deforestation causes: soil erosion, loss of habitat, climate change, reduced rainfall. Conservation methods: national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, biosphere reserves, Project Tiger, Project Elephant. Pollution types: air pollution (vehicles, industries), water pollution (industrial waste, sewage), soil pollution (pesticides, plastic), noise pollution. 3R principle: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle. Non-renewable energy: coal, petroleum, natural gas (fossil fuels). Renewable energy: solar, wind, hydro, geothermal, biomass. India's target: 500 GW renewable energy by 2030. Odisha has significant coal reserves (Talcher coalfields) and potential for solar energy.
